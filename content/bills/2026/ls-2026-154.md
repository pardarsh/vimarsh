# THE MINES AND MINERALS (DEVELOPMENT AND REGULATION) AMENDMENT BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026 amends the 1957 Act to bring mineral bearing lands under central regulation. It restricts State Governments from levying taxes, cesses, or other charges on mineral rights and mineral bearing lands unless compliant with conditions set by the Central Government. It also invalidates uncollected past state levies while clarifying that amounts already collected do not need to be refunded.

## Summary

- Expands the scope of the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act, 1957 to include the regulation of 'mineral bearing lands' alongside mines (Clause 2).
- Defines 'mineral bearing land' as any land containing mineral contents according to parameters prescribed under section 5(2)(a) of the principal Act (Clause 3).
- Inserts a new section 9D prohibiting State Governments from imposing any tax, cess, or similar levy on mineral rights or mineral bearing lands (whether based on quantity, value, royalty, or otherwise) except under conditions or restrictions set by the Central Government (Clause 4).
- Invalidates retroactively ('at all material times') any such state taxes, cesses, or levies that were not recovered or deposited prior to the commencement of the amendment, overriding any court judgment or other law (Clause 4).
- Protects State Governments from having to refund any such tax, cess, or levy that was already deposited or recovered before the Act's commencement (Clause 4).
- Empowers the Central Government to make rules establishing the conditions and restrictions governing state taxation or levies on mineral rights and mineral bearing lands (Clause 5).

## Key provisions

- Clause 2: Amends Section 2 of the principal Act to explicitly add 'and mineral bearing lands' to the declaration of Union regulation.
- Clause 3: Inserts Section 3(ada) defining 'mineral bearing land' based on parameters prescribed under Section 5(2)(a).
- Clause 4: Inserts Section 9D(1) restricting State Governments from levying any tax, cess, or other levy on mineral rights or mineral bearing lands except under Central Government prescribed conditions.
- Clause 4: Inserts Section 9D(2) declaring invalid all unrecovered or undeposited state levies on mineral rights or lands, regardless of any conflicting law, court judgment, decree, or order, while exempting already collected amounts from being refunded.
- Clause 5: Amends Section 13(2) by adding clause (ta) to empower the Central Government to make rules detailing the restrictions on state taxes, cesses, and levies.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill limits state legislative powers over taxing mineral rights and mineral-bearing lands by subjecting such levies to central rules, overriding judicial decisions or other laws to the contrary. Fiscally, State Governments are legally barred from recovering uncollected or pending levies on mineral rights or lands that were not paid before commencement; however, state revenues already collected or deposited prior to commencement are protected from being refunded.
