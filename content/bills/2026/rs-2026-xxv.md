# The Sacred Bond (Parent Care Leave) Bill, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Sacred Bond (Parent Care Leave) Bill, 2026 entitles employees across public and private establishments to up to 45 days of paid parent care leave during their entire service. It allows employees to care for biological, step, adoptive, foster parents, or parents-in-law aged sixty or older during health emergencies and medical crises. The bill also establishes anti-retaliation protections, penalties for employers who wrongfully deny leave, and a grievance redressal mechanism.

## Summary

- Applies to government establishments as well as private establishments employing ten or more employees, covering regular, temporary, contractual, and apprentice workers.
- Grants employees an aggregate entitlement of up to 45 days of parent care leave with full pay and allowances over their entire service period to attend to the needs of a parent aged sixty or older.
- Defines 'parent' broadly to include biological, adoptive, step, and foster parents, as well as parents-in-law.
- Prohibits debiting parent care leave from any other leave accounts and bars cash encashment of unused leave.
- Requires employees to apply with supporting medical records or certificates, while permitting unapproved leave in emergencies provided documentation is submitted within seven working days.
- Directs employers to process leave applications, as far as practicable, within 48 hours and to record written reasons for any rejection based on service exigencies.
- Protects employees against discrimination, retaliation, or denial of career progression such as promotions and increments for availing the leave.
- Imposes a financial penalty ranging from ₹50,000 to ₹2,00,000 on employers who unlawfully deny leave, and subjects employees who submit fraudulent documentation to disciplinary action and salary recovery.
- Establishes a grievance mechanism directing government employees to departmental service rules and private sector workers to the Labour Commissioner, who must decide complaints within 60 days.

## Key provisions

- Clause 2: Defines 'parent' as biological, step, adoptive, foster, or parent-in-law aged 60 or above, and defines 'establishment' to include all government entities and private entities with ten or more employees.
- Clause 3: Entitles employees to a non-encashable aggregate of up to 45 days of fully paid parent care leave during their entire service, which will not be debited against other leave accounts.
- Clause 4: Outlines application procedures requiring medical documentation, sets a 48-hour target for employer disposal with written reasons required for refusals, and permits emergency leave if documentation is provided within seven working days.
- Clause 5: Prohibits employers from subjecting employees to adverse consequences in career progression, promotion, postings, or increments solely for using parent care leave.
- Clause 6: Imposes a penalty between ₹50,000 and ₹2,00,000 on employers for unjustified leave denial or discrimination, and prescribes disciplinary action plus salary refund for employees submitting false documents.
- Clause 7: Designates applicable service rules for government employees and the Labour Commissioner (with a 60-day resolution timeline) for private sector employees to resolve complaints.
- Clause 9: Clarifies that provisions are supplementary to, and not in derogation of, existing leave entitlements.

## Fiscal or legal impact

The bill creates a statutory legal right to paid parent care leave supplementary to existing leave frameworks, binding government bodies and private employers with ten or more employees. It imposes legal liabilities on non-compliant employers, including fines between ₹50,000 and ₹2,00,000 and quasi-judicial scrutiny by Labour Commissioners. The bill contains no Financial Memorandum, meaning direct government fiscal expenditure figures are not stated in the text.
