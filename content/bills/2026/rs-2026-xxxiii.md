# The Free Education (Secondary to Undergraduate Level) Bill, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Free Education (Secondary to Undergraduate Level) Bill, 2026 establishes a legal right for Indian citizens to receive free secondary education (classes 9 to 12) and one free undergraduate degree. It prohibits educational institutions from charging tuition, capitation fees, or compulsory charges, and bars discriminatory admissions screening. The bill also establishes shared fiscal responsibility between the Union and the States through a dedicated national fund.

## Summary

- Grants eligible Indian citizens who have completed Class 8 the right to free secondary education (Classes 9 to 12) in a neighbourhood educational institution.
- Grants eligible Indian citizens who have completed Class 12 the right to pursue one free undergraduate degree programme in any recognised institution.
- Prohibits denial of admission at both levels based on caste, religion, gender, disability, language, socio-economic background, migration status, or place of birth.
- Obligates appropriate governments to provide necessary infrastructure, maintain prescribed pupil-teacher ratios, furnish free textbooks, digital resources, and support facilities for persons with disabilities and disadvantaged categories.
- Directs governments to progressively expand higher education capacity, fund institutions, and provide scholarships, hostels, transport, and academic support.
- Prohibits educational institutions from collecting capitation fees, imposing compulsory charges, or conducting discriminatory screening inconsistent with merit and constitutional principles.
- Establishes concurrent responsibility between the Central and State Governments to fund the implementation, authorising the creation of a National Fund for Universal Secondary and Higher Education.

## Key provisions

- Clause 3: Guarantees every eligible person the right to free secondary education in neighbourhood schools, bars discriminatory denial of admission, and mandates completion certificates.
- Clause 4: Sets statutory duties for the appropriate Government to ensure adequate neighbourhood secondary schools, qualified teachers, free learning materials, digital resources, and disability support.
- Clause 5: Confers a statutory right to pursue one free undergraduate education programme in recognised institutions without discrimination.
- Clause 6: Requires the appropriate Government to establish, maintain, and fund higher educational institutions, expand capacity, and provide scholarships, transport, and hostels.
- Clause 7: Expressly prohibits capitation fees, any other compulsory charges, and discriminatory screening procedures.
- Clause 8: Mandates concurrent funding responsibilities between Central and State Governments and allows establishment of the National Fund for Universal Secondary and Higher Education.
- Clause 9 and 11: Grants rule-making powers to appropriate governments and contains a power to remove difficulties by executive order.

## Fiscal or legal impact

The Financial Memorandum states that enacting the bill will involve recurring and non-recurring expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India and the Consolidated Funds of the States, though the exact quantum cannot be quantified at this stage. Legally, the bill establishes enforceable rights to free secondary and undergraduate education, imposes affirmative statutory duties on Central and State Governments, strictly bans capitation fees and screening charges, and operates in addition to, rather than in derogation of, existing laws.
