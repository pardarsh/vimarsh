# THE INDIAN STATISTICAL INSTITUTE BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Indian Statistical Institute Bill, 2026 seeks to reconstitute the Indian Statistical Institute (ISI) from a registered society into a statutory body corporate of national importance. It establishes a modernized governance structure featuring a Board of Governors, Academic Council, and Centre-level Management Councils. The bill also repeals the Indian Statistical Institute Act, 1959 while maintaining the institution's status of national importance.

## Summary

- Reconstitutes the existing Indian Statistical Institute from a society under the West Bengal Societies Registration Act, 1961 into a statutory body corporate with perpetual succession (Clauses 3 and 4).
- Repeals the Indian Statistical Institute Act, 1959, while preserving the provision declaring the Institute to be an institution of national importance (Clause 47 and Schedule).
- Establishes a governance framework designating the President of India as the Visitor, alongside a Board of Governors as the principal executive body, an Academic Council, and Management Councils for individual Centres (Clauses 12, 13, 14, 18, and 20).
- Specifies leadership positions and administrative structures, including the Director as Chief Executive Officer, Centre-Directors, Deans, and the Registrar (Clauses 22, 23, 24, and 25).
- Empowers the Institute to grant degrees, establish campuses or centres in India and abroad, determine fees, hold property, promote start-ups, and receive grants and generate revenue (Clauses 6, 11, and 28).
- Mandates non-discriminatory admissions based on merit, establishes status as a Central Educational Institution for reservation purposes, and specifies the Institute shall function as a not-for-profit legal entity (Clauses 7 and 8).
- Establishes financial and audit mechanisms, creating a dedicated Fund and corpus fund, requiring auditing by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG), and mandating that audited reports be laid before Parliament (Clauses 29 and 30).
- Institutes periodic accountability reviews of the Institute and faculty by an independent agency or expert group every five years, with reports published publicly and submitted to the Central Government (Clauses 17 and 36).
- Explicitly subjects the Institute to the Right to Information Act, 2005 (Clause 41).

## Key provisions

- Clause 3 & 4: Incorporates the Institute as a body corporate, transferring all existing properties, rights, liabilities, employees, and ongoing student enrollments to the new corporate entity.
- Clause 7: Mandates admissions on merit while complying with the Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006, and prohibits discrimination based on gender, race, creed, caste, or class.
- Clause 13: Appoints the President of India as Visitor, with authority to direct inquiries, review work, and issue binding directions.
- Clause 14: Details the composition of the Board of Governors, which includes government nominees, eminent persons, the Director, a rotating Centre-Director, and faculty representatives.
- Clause 20: Provides for establishing autonomous Management Councils at individual Institute Centres to oversee day-to-day administrative and operational matters.
- Clause 22 & 23: Outlines five-year terms for the Director and Centre-Directors, detailing procedures for appointments, emergent decision-making, and removals.
- Clause 30 & 32: Mandates internal audits, audit committees, and statutory auditing by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India, whose reports must be tabled in Parliament.
- Clause 34: Requires the annual financial report to disclose the five highest-paid employees and specifically declare any familial relationship to members of the Board or Academic Council.
- Clause 36: Requires institutional and faculty performance reviews by an independent agency at least once every five years.
- Clause 47: Formally repeals the Indian Statistical Institute Act, 1959, while preserving its declaration of national importance in the Schedule.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill dissolves the society status of the existing Institute and establishes it as a statutory body corporate, repealing the 1959 Act while maintaining its status of national importance, and bringing it under the Right to Information Act, 2005 and CAG audit jurisdiction. Fiscally, according to the Financial Memorandum, expenditures will continue to be met from existing budgetary provisions made by the Central Government, involving no additional recurring or non-recurring expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India.
