# THE TAXATION AND OTHER LAWS (AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026 amends the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, the Income-tax Act, 2025, and the Finance Act, 2026. It updates electronic payment specifications, clarifies 'safe harbour' business connection rules for offshore funds and their managers, and introduces tax exemptions for select foreign entities, contract manufacturing, diamond trade, and investments in government securities. It also adjusts domestic corporate surcharge rates and repeals the Income-tax (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026.

## Summary

- Amends the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 by empowering the Central Government to notify electronic modes of payment directly instead of tying them to Section 269SU of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (Clause 2).
- Substitutes Schedule I of the Income-tax Act, 2025 to update the qualifying criteria for eligible investment funds and fund managers so their activities do not trigger a taxable business connection in India (Clause 3).
- Expands Schedule IV exemptions under the Income-tax Act, 2025 to include contract manufacturing of specified electronic goods through 2040-2041 (Clause 4(a)).
- Exempts interest on Government securities and capital gains arising from their transfer when earned by Foreign Institutional Investors and the Bank for International Settlements (Clause 4(c)).
- Introduces tax exemptions effective October 1, 2026 through March 31, 2041 on rough diamond sales in notified special zones by foreign mining or trading entities, and on warehouse component storage in custom bonded areas used for manufacturing specified electronic goods (Clause 4(d)).
- Revises surcharge rates under the Finance Act, 2026 by distinguishing domestic special purpose vehicles (taxed at a 25% surcharge) from other domestic companies (taxed at a 10% surcharge) under specified tax sections (Clause 6).
- Repeals the Income-tax (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026 while saving actions taken under it (Clause 7).

## Key provisions

- Clause 2: Amends Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 to allow the Central Government to notify electronic payment modes.
- Clause 3: Replaces Schedule I of the Income-tax Act, 2025, setting out residency, investment cap limits (5% direct Indian resident corpus limit with a 4-month cure period), manager independence, and reporting requirements for offshore funds to avoid constituting a business connection.
- Clause 4(a) & 4(e): Amends entry 13A of Schedule IV of the Income-tax Act, 2025 to extend the exemption period to tax year 2040-2041 and defines 'specified electronic goods' to include phones, laptops, tablets, servers, sub-assemblies, and wearables.
- Clause 4(c): Adds entries 13D and 13E in Schedule IV to exempt interest and capital gains on Government securities for Foreign Institutional Investors and the Bank for International Settlements.
- Clause 4(d): Adds entries 13F and 13G in Schedule IV (effective October 1, 2026) exempting rough diamond sales in notified zones and custom-bonded warehouse storage of components for electronic goods manufacturing until March 31, 2041.
- Clause 6: Amends Section 3 of the Finance Act, 2026 to set a 10% surcharge rate for domestic companies generally and 25% for domestic companies that are special purpose vehicles under Section 200 or 201.
- Clause 7: Repeals the Income-tax (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026 (Ord. 2 of 2026).

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the Act retroactively amends the Income-tax Act, 2025 and the Finance Act, 2026 with effect from April 1, 2026 (save for specific provisions commencing upon notification or on October 1, 2026), and formally repeals Ordinance 2 of 2026. Fiscally, the Act provides tax exemptions on specific capital gains, interest, rough diamond trade, and electronic component warehousing, while bifurcating domestic corporate surcharges between standard domestic companies (10%) and special purpose vehicles (25%).
