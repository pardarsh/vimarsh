# THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIRST AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-first Amendment) Bill, 2026 seeks to lift the post-2026 freeze on seat delimitation and increases the maximum membership of the Lok Sabha to 850. It enables Parliament to determine which published census will form the basis for readjusting legislative constituencies through the Delimitation Commission. Additionally, it modifies the timeline and scope for implementing women's seat reservations, extending the provisions to Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir.

## Summary

- Increases the maximum ceiling of Lok Sabha members from 550 to 850 (up to 815 from States and up to 35 from Union territories).
- Removes the constitutional freeze that delayed fresh delimitation until the publication of the first census taken after 2026, omitting the third provisos in Articles 82 and 170(3).
- Permits Parliament by law to specify the published census figures to be used for determining population in Articles 55, 81, 170, and 330.
- Mandates that the readjustment of legislative seats and constituencies be conducted by the Delimitation Commission based on the census determined by Parliament.
- Revises Scheduled Tribe reservation calculations upon readjustment of seats in the Legislative Assemblies of Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, and Tripura.
- Substitutes Article 334A to allow women's reservations in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies to take effect after an initial delimitation exercise, without waiting for the first census after the 2023 Amendment Act, and explicitly includes the Legislative Assemblies of Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir.

## Key provisions

- Clause 2 amends the Explanation to Article 55 to define 'population' for presidential elections as ascertained at such census as Parliament may determine under Article 82 or 170.
- Clause 3 amends Article 81(1) to increase the maximum strength of the Lok Sabha to 815 members from States and 35 from Union territories, and substitutes Clause (3) regarding the definition of population.
- Clause 4 amends Article 82 by omitting the third proviso (post-2026 freeze) and specifying that readjustment shall be carried out by the Delimitation Commission based on a census determined by law.
- Clause 5 amends Article 170 by removing the reference to Article 333, omitting the third proviso of Clause (3) which barred readjustment until a post-2026 census, and specifying the Delimitation Commission as the readjustment authority.
- Clause 6 amends the Explanation under Article 330(3) to align the definition of population for Articles 330 and 332 with the census determined by Parliament.
- Clause 7 amends Article 332(3A) and (3B) to provide formulas for preserving Scheduled Tribe seat proportions in the Legislative Assemblies of Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, and Tripura following seat readjustments.
- Clause 8 substitutes Article 334A to operationalise women's reservation following a delimitation exercise for that purpose, extending coverage to the Union territories of Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir alongside Delhi and the States, with seats rotating and expiring after 15 years unless extended by Parliament.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill amends the Constitution of India (Articles 55, 81, 82, 170, 330, 332, and 334A), removing the long-standing freeze on the reallocation of parliamentary and assembly seats and empowering the Delimitation Commission to alter seat counts and boundaries based on a census chosen by Parliament. It also amends the operational trigger for implementing one-third reservation of seats for women. The bill text contains no Financial Memorandum and states no direct fiscal appropriations.
