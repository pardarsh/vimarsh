# THE FOREIGN CONTRIBUTION (REGULATION) AMENDMENT BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026 amends the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 to establish a comprehensive statutory mechanism for the vesting, management, and disposal of foreign contributions and assets of defunct, non-renewed, or cancelled entities through a Designated Authority. It rationalises criminal penalties for unauthorised acceptance or utilisation of foreign funds by lowering the maximum prison term from five years to one year. Additionally, the Bill mandates prior approval from the Central Government before any criminal investigation can be initiated under the Act.

## Summary

- Updates references to older statutes by substituting the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, Indian Penal Code, 1860, Indian Evidence Act, 1872, and Companies Act, 1956 with their updated enactments: Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, and Companies Act, 2013.
- Introduces formal definitions for 'Administrator', 'Designated authority', and 'key functionary'—covering directors, partners, trustees, kartas, office-bearers, and controlling officers.
- Clarifies that prior permissions are valid for specific purposes or amounts and requires funds to be received and utilised within a prescribed period.
- Prohibits entities under suspension from alienating, encumbering, or dealing with assets created from foreign contributions without prior approval from the Central Government.
- Inserts Section 14B defining when registration certificates are deemed to have ceased (expiry, non-renewal, or refused renewal) and prohibits receiving or utilising foreign contribution once ceased.
- Replaces Section 15 and Section 22 with Chapter IIIA, setting up provisional vesting of assets and funds into a Designated Authority upon cancellation, surrender, or cessation, which can become permanent if registration is not restored or renewed.
- Outlines duties of the Designated Authority to manage, safeguard, or sell vested assets for public purposes (crediting proceeds to the Consolidated Fund of India), while mandating that places of worship have their religious character maintained and their management entrusted to appropriate persons.
- Empowers the Designated Authority with Civil Court powers under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, establishes procedures for appeals to District Judges, and provides that sale certificates issued by the Designated Authority serve as conclusive proof of ownership without needing original title deeds.
- Substitutes Section 35 to lower the maximum term of imprisonment for illegal acceptance, utilisation, or assistance in receiving foreign contribution from five years to one year (or fine, or both).
- Amends Section 43 to require mandatory prior approval from the Central Government before initiating any investigation for offences under the Act.

## Key provisions

- Clause 3: Inserts definitions for 'Administrator', 'Designated authority', and 'key functionary' in Section 2(1).
- Clause 5: Amends Section 12 to specify that prior permission shall be valid for a specific purpose or amount and must be received and utilised within a prescribed period.
- Clause 7: Inserts clause (c) in Section 13(2), preventing suspended entities from alienating or encumbering assets created from foreign contributions without prior Central Government approval.
- Clause 9: Inserts Section 14B establishing conditions under which a registration certificate is deemed to have ceased upon expiry or refusal of renewal.
- Clause 11: Inserts Chapter IIIA (Sections 16A to 16L), creating a statutory framework for provisional and permanent vesting of foreign funds and assets in a Designated Authority, empowering the authority to manage, transfer, or sell assets and credit sale proceeds to the Consolidated Fund of India, while setting out appeal procedures to District Judges.
- Clause 16: Substitutes Section 35, amending the penal provision to prescribe punishment of imprisonment up to one year, or fine, or both, for contraventions regarding the acceptance or utilisation of foreign contributions.
- Clause 17: Substitutes Section 39 to attribute liability to 'key functionaries' for offences committed by persons other than individuals.
- Clause 18: Amends Section 43 by adding sub-section (2), requiring prior approval of the Central Government before initiating any investigation under the Act.

## Fiscal or legal impact

According to the Financial Memorandum, the Bill does not involve any expenditure, recurring or non-recurring, from the Consolidated Fund of India; any proceeds from the sale of permanently vested assets and unutilised foreign contributions will be credited into the Consolidated Fund of India. Legally, it centralises asset custody with a Designated Authority, requires prior Central Government approval to initiate criminal investigations under the Act, expands personal liability across non-individual entities to defined 'key functionaries', and reduces the maximum prison sentence under Section 35 from five years to one year.
