# The Anti-Racism and Equality Bill, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Anti-Racism and Equality Bill, 2026, aims to define, prohibit, and penalize acts of racial discrimination and racial harassment. It introduces designated Special Courts, establishes statutory rights and protections for victims and witnesses, and creates criminal penalties for individuals, establishments, and negligent public servants.

## Summary

- Defines 'race' broadly to encompass race, colour, ethnicity, nationality, descent, caste, tribe, language, accent, and regional or cultural identity, whether real or imputed.
- Prohibits persons and establishments from engaging in, promoting, facilitating, abetting, condoning, or permitting racial discrimination or harassment.
- Imposes criminal penalties ranging from up to two or three years imprisonment for harassment or discrimination, rising up to seven years if the offence leads to serious bodily injury, severe trauma, social boycott, loss of livelihood, or other severe harms, or in cases of subsequent convictions.
- Makes all offences under the Bill cognizable and non-bailable, requiring investigations to be led by an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police.
- Mandates the designation of one or more Courts of Session as Special Courts in each district to conclude trials within six months to one year, shifting the burden to the accused by establishing a presumption of a culpable mental state.
- Grants extensive victim and witness protections, including free medical and psychological care, interpretation services, mandated family notification, protective safe shelter, and legal support through Special Public Prosecutors.
- Penalizes public servants with up to two years of imprisonment and fines for wilful neglect of duties under the Act, and establishes corporate liability for key managerial personnel of offending establishments.

## Key provisions

- Clause 2: Defines race, racial discrimination, racial harassment, racially motivated acts, and establishments.
- Clause 3 & 4: Prohibits racial discrimination and harassment; prescribes imprisonment up to 3 years for discrimination and up to 2 years for harassment, increasing to 3 to 7 years with fine for aggravated harms; specifies offences are cognizable, non-bailable, and investigated by officers at or above the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police.
- Clause 5: Establishes establishment liability, making in-charge persons and complicit directors, managers, or officers criminally liable unless due diligence or lack of knowledge is proven.
- Clause 6: Punishes public servants with up to 2 years of imprisonment and fine for wilful neglect of prescribed duties like case registration or victim protection.
- Clause 7: Mandates enhanced punishment up to 7 years imprisonment for repeat convictions.
- Clause 8: Enumerates victim and witness rights, such as safety from intimidation, privacy, informational updates, and protection against secondary victimisation.
- Clauses 9 & 10: Mandates designation of Courts of Session as Special Courts in each district, directing them to endeavour to complete trials within 6 months (extendable up to one year with recorded reasons) and empowering them to award victim compensation.
- Clause 11: Places a statutory presumption of a culpable mental state (intention, motive, or knowledge) upon the accused.
- Clause 12: Requires the appointment of experienced Special Public Prosecutors with at least 7 years of practice.
- Clauses 14–18: Imposes police obligations regarding translation, intimation of family, free medical treatment, mental health support, and protective safe custody.
- Clause 20 & 21: Requires periodic police training/sensitization and designates non-compliance by police or authorities as actionable misconduct under service rules.
- Clause 25: Mandates the Central Government to provide requisite funds to State Governments to implement the Act.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill creates new cognizable, non-bailable criminal offences, places the burden of rebutting a culpable mental state on the accused, and overrides any conflicting statutory provisions to the extent of inconsistency while functioning in addition to other laws. Fiscally, according to the Financial Memorandum, the bill entails an estimated non-recurring expenditure of approximately Rs. 200 crore for infrastructure upgrades and sensitization modules, alongside an estimated recurring expenditure of around Rs. 75 crore per annum for prosecutor fees and victim relief or compensation.
