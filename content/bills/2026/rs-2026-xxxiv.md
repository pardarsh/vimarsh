# The Central Nursing University Bill, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Central Nursing University Bill, 2026 seeks to establish and incorporate a Central Nursing University headquartered at Kannur in Kerala. It aims to advance nursing education, research, clinical training, and leadership development across undergraduate, postgraduate, doctoral, and postdoctoral levels. The university will operate with an all-India jurisdiction and may establish campuses and study centres both within India and abroad.

## Summary

- Establishes 'The Central Nursing University' as a body corporate headquartered in Kannur, Kerala, with nationwide jurisdiction and the power to set up outlying campuses and study centres in India and abroad with Central Government approval (Clauses 3 and 6).
- Defines the university's core objects, which include promoting multidisciplinary nursing education, developing advanced clinical practitioners and nurse educators, conducting healthcare and policy research, and serving as a national resource centre (Clause 4).
- Designates the President of India as the Visitor and outlines the primary officers, including the Chancellor, Vice-Chancellor, Pro-Vice-Chancellor, Deans of Schools, Registrar, Finance Officer, and Controller of Examinations (Clauses 8 to 18).
- Constitutes major university authorities including the Court, Executive Council (with adequate representation of women), Academic Council, Board of Studies, and Finance Committee (Clauses 19 to 25).
- Establishes a University Fund composed of government contributions, internal revenues, donations, and other receipts, requiring prior Central Government approval for any foreign funding (Clause 26).
- Mandates an all-India admissions and faculty recruitment process based on merit, while forbidding discrimination on grounds of caste, creed, race, or sex, and explicitly barring special provisions based on domicile while allowing reservations for women, persons with disabilities, SC, ST, and OBCs (Clauses 5 and 7).
- Provides dispute resolution mechanisms for employees via an Arbitration Tribunal, whose decisions are final and exclude civil court suits, while preserving constitutional remedies under Articles 32 and 226 (Clause 33).
- Requires annual financial auditing by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India and mandates laying the annual report, audit report, statutes, ordinances, regulations, and difficulty-removal orders before both Houses of Parliament (Clauses 30, 31, 46, and 47).
- Sets transitional provisions allowing the Visitor to appoint the first Chancellor, Vice-Chancellor, Registrar, and Finance Officer, and the Central Government to nominate members of the first Court, Executive Council, and Academic Council for up to three years (Clause 48).

## Key provisions

- Establishment and Incorporation (Clause 3): Creates the Central Nursing University in Kannur, Kerala, with perpetual succession, common seal, and power to establish outlying campuses in India and abroad with prior Central Government approval.
- Non-Discrimination and Reservations (Clause 7): Ensures the university is open to all persons regardless of sex, caste, creed, race, or class; permits special provisions for women, persons with disabilities, SCs, STs, and OBCs, but expressly prohibits domicile-based preferences.
- Visitor Powers and Governance Framework (Clauses 8-11 and 19-22): Designates the President of India as Visitor; sets up the Court, Academic Council, and the Executive Council (which must have an adequate number of women members).
- Mandatory All-India Standards (Clause 5(2)): Directs nationwide student admissions based on merit or Common Entrance Tests, inter-university mobility of faculty, choice-based credit systems, NAAC accreditation, and e-governance.
- Finances and Audit (Clauses 26 and 31): Sets up the University Fund, directs the institution to strive for financial self-sufficiency, requires prior approval for foreign funding, and mandates audits by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India.
- Employee Dispute Resolution (Clause 33): Requires written contracts for staff and sets an Arbitration Tribunal to handle employment disputes; bars civil courts while safeguarding access to Articles 32 and 226 of the Constitution.
- Student and Employee Right of Appeal (Clause 34): Provides employees and students the right to appeal decisions of university officers or affiliated institution authorities to the Executive Council.
- Parliamentary Scrutiny (Clauses 30, 31, and 47): Mandates that the annual report, audited accounts, statutes, ordinances, regulations, and any difficulty-removal orders be laid before both Houses of Parliament.

## Fiscal or legal impact

According to the Financial Memorandum, the establishment of the university is expected to require about 1,000 crore rupees during the next Plan period, funded from the Consolidated Fund of India through the University Grants Commission under the Ministry of Education's budget. Legally, the Bill creates a statutory body corporate under central law that operates in addition to, and not in derogation of, the University Grants Commission Act, 1956. It restricts civil court jurisdiction over employment disputes in favour of a statutory Arbitration Tribunal under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, while expressly retaining constitutional writ remedies under Articles 32 and 226.
