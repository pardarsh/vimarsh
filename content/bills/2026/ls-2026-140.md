# THE REGISTRATION OF BIRTHS AND DEATHS (AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026 amends section 13 of the parent Act regarding the registration of delayed births and deaths. It establishes different approving authorities depending on the length of the delay. Registration delayed between one and two years requires an order from an Executive Magistrate, while delays exceeding two years require an order from a Judicial Magistrate of the first class.

## Summary

- Amends section 13(3) of the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969.
- Provides that reporting a birth or death after one year but within two years requires an order from a District Magistrate, Sub-Divisional Magistrate, or an authorised Executive Magistrate.
- Defines Executive Magistrate by reference to section 14(1) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023.
- Introduces sub-section (3A), specifying that reporting a birth or death delayed by more than two years requires an order from a Judicial Magistrate of the first class.
- Requires verification of the correctness of the event and payment of prescribed fees for both delay categories.
- Comes into force on a date appointed by the Central Government via the Official Gazette.

## Key provisions

- Clause 1(2): States that the Act comes into force on a date appointed by the Central Government via notification in the Official Gazette.
- Clause 2 [substituting Section 13(3)]: Mandates that delayed registration between one and two years of occurrence can only be made on an order of a District Magistrate, Sub-Divisional Magistrate, or an authorised Executive Magistrate upon verifying correctness and paying prescribed fees.
- Clause 2 [Explanation to Section 13(3)]: Defines 'Executive Magistrate' as one appointed under section 14(1) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023.
- Clause 2 [inserting Section 13(3A)]: Mandates that registration delayed by more than two years requires an order from a Judicial Magistrate of the first class following verification and fee payment.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill bifurcates the procedure for delayed registration of births and deaths based on the duration of delay, shifting authority for delays between one and two years to administrative/executive magistrates and reserving delays exceeding two years for Judicial Magistrates of the first class. It updates legal references to align with the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023. The bill text contains no Financial Memorandum and states no direct fiscal impact.
