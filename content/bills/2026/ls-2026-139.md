# THE PUBLIC EXAMINATIONS (PREVENTION OF UNFAIR MEANS) AMENDMENT BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026 enhances penalties and fines for offences related to unfair means in public examinations. It enables the creation of a Special Task Force for investigations, introduces a two-month investigation timeline, and mandates designated Special Fast Track Courts for speedy trials. Additionally, it sets strict timeframes for trials and High Court appeals, including appeals against bail orders.

## Summary

- Substantially increases the terms of imprisonment and financial penalties across sections 10 and 11 of the 2024 Act for individuals, service providers, and management involved in examination malpractice.
- Authorizes the Central Government to constitute a Special Task Force for investigation, establishing that once constituted, investigations will be handled exclusively by it.
- Imposes a strict two-month deadline for completing investigations by empowered officers, central agencies, or the Special Task Force.
- Requires States and Union Territories to designate Courts of Session as Special Fast Track Courts, in consultation with the Chief Justice of the respective High Court, to conduct trials on a day-to-day basis.
- Mandates that trials in Special Fast Track Courts conclude within three months from the filing of the chargesheet or from the date of transfer of pending cases.
- Requires the appointment of Special Public Prosecutors for every Special Fast Track Court.
- Provides that appeals from Special Fast Track Court judgments or bail orders will be heard directly by a two-judge bench of the High Court, generally to be disposed of within three months.

## Key provisions

- Clause 2(a): Increases the general punishment in section 10(1) from 3–5 years imprisonment to 5–10 years, and raises the fine up to fifty lakh rupees (previously ten lakh rupees).
- Clause 2(b)–(c): In section 10(2), increases the fine for service providers from up to one crore rupees to up to five crore rupees and debars involvement for eight years instead of four; in section 10(3), raises imprisonment for key management persons to a minimum of five years and fine up to five crore rupees.
- Clause 3: Increases the minimum imprisonment under section 11(1) from five years to seven years and the minimum fine from one crore rupees to ten crore rupees.
- Clause 4: Amends section 12(2) allowing the Central Government to notify a Special Task Force that has sole jurisdiction to investigate offences referred to it.
- Clause 5 (New Section 12A): Requires investigations to be completed within two months; designates Special Fast Track Courts for day-to-day trials to be completed within three months from the chargesheet; transfers pending cases to these courts; and directs the appointment of Special Public Prosecutors.
- Clause 5 (New Section 12B): Establishes that appeals against judgments or orders (including bail decisions, but barring interlocutory orders) lie directly to a two-judge bench of the High Court, must be filed within 30 days (extendable up to 90 days for cause), and should be disposed of within three months.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill amends the 2024 Act by steepening criminal penalties, imposing strict procedural timelines on investigations and trials, overriding standard procedures in the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 for appeals (including direct High Court appeals for bail orders), and transferring existing pending trials to designated fast-track courts. The text does not include a Financial Memorandum or explicitly specify the fiscal costs associated with designating Special Fast Track Courts, constituting Special Task Forces, or appointing Special Public Prosecutors.
