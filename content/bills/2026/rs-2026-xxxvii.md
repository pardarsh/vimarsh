# The Indian Diaspora and Migrant Workers (Protection and Welfare) Bill, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Indian Diaspora and Migrant Workers (Protection and Welfare) Bill, 2026 establishes a statutory framework to safeguard Non-Resident Indians and migrant workers abroad from exploitation, recruitment fraud, and human trafficking. It creates the National Commission for Welfare of Indian Diaspora and Migrant Workers to facilitate legal aid, grievance redressal, emergency assistance, and state-funded repatriation. The bill also mandates the appointment of nodal officers within domestic governments and Indian diplomatic missions to coordinate diaspora welfare and legal proceedings.

## Summary

- Defines key terms including distressed persons, migrant workers (encompassing victims of recruitment fraud, trafficking, or commercial exploitation), and the Indian diaspora (NRIs, OCIs, and PIOs) in a welfare-oriented capacity.
- Grants statutory rights to diaspora members and migrant workers to access protection, legal information, property protection in India, and protection against the confiscation of travel documents, alongside the right to state-funded repatriation in cases of employer insolvency or abuse.
- Establishes the National Commission for Welfare of Indian Diaspora and Migrant Workers, detailing its corporate status, composition (Chairperson, four expert members, and a Member-Secretary), and terms of service.
- Vests the Commission with powers of a civil court to summon information and examine records for inquiry, and empowers it to monitor recruiting agencies, blacklist fraudulent foreign employers or agents, and recommend Look Out Circulars, passport impounding, or prosecution.
- Creates a multi-channel grievance redressal framework allowing complaints to be submitted digitally, physically, or via diplomatic missions without disqualification for technical deficiencies, backed by preliminary assessments and follow-up monitoring.
- Introduces procedural facilitations for Indian diaspora members involved in Indian disputes, including participation via video conferencing, assistance in serving summons, and safeguards prohibiting arbitrary coercive actions.
- Requires every Indian Embassy, High Commission, or Consulate, as well as the Central and State Governments, to designate nodal officers as administrative points of contact for diaspora issues.
- Mandates an annual activity and audit report by the Commission, audited by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India, to be laid before Parliament along with an action-taken memorandum.

## Key provisions

- Clause 3: Establishes statutory rights for migrant workers, including entitlement to state-funded repatriation and protection against employer confiscation of travel documents.
- Clause 5 and Clause 21: Requires appropriate governments and Indian foreign missions (Embassies, High Commissions, and Consulates) to designate nodal officers to act as single-point contacts for diaspora welfare and coordination.
- Clause 6 and Clause 7: Sets up the National Commission for Welfare of Indian Diaspora and Migrant Workers consisting of a Chairperson with 15+ years of relevant experience, four expert members, and a Member-Secretary.
- Clause 10 and Clause 11: Authorises the Commission to maintain a blacklist of fraudulent foreign employers and agents, coordinate rescues and repatriation, examine records with civil court powers under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, and recommend Look Out Circulars or passport impounding.
- Clause 12: Permits distressed NRIs, migrant workers, or their families to file grievances in physical or digital form or through missions abroad, barring rejection based on language, format, or technical defects.
- Clause 16: Mandates procedural accommodation by allowing the diaspora to participate in proceedings via video conferencing, prohibiting physical absence from being treated as default.
- Clause 23 and Clause 24: Directs CAG auditing of Commission accounts and requires the Central Government to lay the Commission's annual report and action-taken memorandum before Parliament.
- Clause 27: Specifies that the Act operates in addition to existing laws, but holds overriding effect to the extent of any inconsistency.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill establishes a national statutory body with civil court inquiry powers under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, overrides conflicting provisions in other laws to the extent of inconsistency (Clause 27), provides good-faith immunity to officials (Clause 28), and introduces procedural rights such as digital participation in civil disputes. Fiscally, according to the Financial Memorandum, the bill entails an estimated non-recurring expenditure of approximately Rs. 10 crore for infrastructural setup and an estimated recurring expenditure of approximately Rs. 50 crore annually from the Consolidated Fund of India for salaries, state-funded repatriations, and training programs.
