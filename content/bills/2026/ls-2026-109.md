# THE UNION TERRITORIES LAWS (AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 amends the governance acts for Puducherry, Delhi, and Jammu and Kashmir to align their delimitation, constituency readjustment, and population definitions with applicable delimitation law. It empowers the Delimitation Commission to determine seat strengths and boundaries subject to statutory floors, while linking women's legislative reservation to Article 334A and delimitation. It also establishes transitional safeguards ensuring existing assembly representations and constituency boundaries remain valid until formal readjustments take effect.

## Summary

- Amends the Government of Union Territories Act, 1963 to formally retitle and confine its scope primarily to the Union territory of Puducherry, substituting 'Pondicherry' with 'Puducherry' throughout.
- Provides that the direct election seats in the Legislative Assemblies of Puducherry (minimum 30), Delhi (minimum 70), and Jammu and Kashmir (minimum 114) shall be determined by the Delimitation Commission under applicable delimitation law.
- Revises the statutory definition of 'population' across the three Acts to match the definition provided under the applicable law on delimitation.
- Harmonises women's reservation in Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir with Article 334A of the Constitution and applicable delimitation law, omitting standalone sunset and enforcement clauses.
- Adjusts nominated member caps: allows Puducherry's nominations to increase to five (including two women) and Jammu and Kashmir's nominations (for women and Kashmiri migrants) to increase to three each, effective only after constituency readjustment under delimitation law takes effect.
- Maintains that 24 seats in the Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly remain vacant for Pakistan-occupied areas and are excluded from the Assembly's total membership count.
- Repeals obsolete, historical delimitation and transitional clauses across all three Acts, including old sections referencing past censuses (such as 2001 and 2011) and defunct territories like Goa, Daman and Diu, Mizoram, and Arunachal Pradesh.
- Includes transitional provisions stating that constituency readjustments will not alter existing Legislative Assembly representations until dissolution, allowing interim elections under existing territorial boundaries.

## Key provisions

- Clauses 2-5: Amends the long title, short title, and Part II heading of the Government of Union Territories Act, 1963 to specifically reflect 'Puducherry' instead of general Union territories or 'Pondicherry'.
- Clauses 6, 15, and 19: Sets statutory minimum direct election seats at 30 for Puducherry, 70 for Delhi, and 114 for Jammu & Kashmir, delegating final determination of seat counts and territorial constituencies to the Delimitation Commission.
- Clauses 6, 15, and 19: Replaces fixed census references with the provision that 'population' shall have the meaning given under the applicable law on delimitation.
- Clauses 6, 22, and 23: Increases post-delimitation nominated members to five (with two reserved for women) in Puducherry, and up to three members each for women's representation and Kashmiri Migrants in Jammu & Kashmir.
- Clauses 7, 8, 20, and 21: Aligns women's reservation rules in Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir with delimitation laws and Article 334A of the Constitution, repealing sections 3B (Puducherry) and 14B (J&K).
- Clauses 11, 16, and 24: Inserts and substitutes provisions (Section 40A in 1963 Act, Section 38 in 1991 Act, Section 60 in 2019 Act) placing assembly and parliamentary readjustment under the Delimitation Commission, protecting existing assemblies from dissolution-triggered changes and safeguarding intervening elections.
- Clauses 11, 13, 14, 17, and 24: Repeals outdated statutory provisions, including procedural delimitation sections, obsolete schedules, and historical administration sections for other former Union territories.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill aligns statutory provisions in the Union territories of Puducherry, Delhi, and Jammu & Kashmir with the revised constitutional frameworks of the Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023 and the proposed Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-first Amendment) Bill, 2026 regarding seat determination, women's reservation, and delimitation. It vests constituency readjustment power uniformly in the Delimitation Commission and repeals obsolete historical provisions. The bill contains no financial memorandum specifying direct expenditures, and the Statement of Objects and Reasons notes that the amendments are consequential and enabling in nature.
