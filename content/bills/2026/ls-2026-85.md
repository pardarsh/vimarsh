# THE CORPORATE LAWS (AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Corporate Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 amends the Companies Act, 2013 and the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 to ease corporate compliance and decriminalise several procedural defaults into civil penalties. It introduces frameworks for entities operating in International Financial Services Centres, permits specified trusts to convert into LLPs, and allows hybrid or virtual general meetings. Additionally, it strengthens the regulatory framework by expanding the powers of the National Financial Reporting Authority and designating the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India as the Valuation Authority.

## Summary

- Amends the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 to accommodate entities operating in International Financial Services Centres (IFSCs), including enabling contribution accounting in permitted foreign currencies and relaxing filing timelines for entities regulated by SEBI or IFSCA.
- Inserts provisions enabling 'specified trusts' (such as registered Alternative Investment Funds) to convert directly into Limited Liability Partnerships under a newly inserted Fifth Schedule.
- Decriminalises multiple minor and procedural offences across both Acts, substituting criminal fines and prosecutions with civil monetary penalties adjudicated by appointed officers.
- Broadens the definition of 'small company' by increasing maximum threshold limits to a paid-up capital of up to twenty crore rupees and turnover of up to two hundred crore rupees.
- Enables companies to hold Annual General Meetings (AGMs) and Extraordinary General Meetings (EGMs) through video conferencing or hybrid modes, subject to holding an in-person physical AGM at least once every three years.
- Rationalises corporate governance and corporate social responsibility (CSR) rules, including exempting prescribed small companies from auditor appointments and easing CSR expenditure timelines and committee thresholds.
- Recognises the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) as the statutory Valuation Authority and establishes the National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA) as a body corporate with an independent fund and expanded supervisory powers.
- Streamlines merger and corporate reorganization procedures, designating a single bench of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) based on the transferee company's jurisdiction and setting a three-year deadline to dispose of or cancel certain shares held in trust or treasury.

## Key provisions

- Clause 2 & 27: Defines and establishes rules for IFSC-based LLPs and companies, allowing them to issue and maintain share capital or partner contributions in permitted foreign currencies while requiring fee and penalty payments in Indian rupees.
- Clause 12, 13 & 17: Inserts Section 57A and the Fifth Schedule into the LLP Act, permitting the conversion of SEBI- or IFSCA-registered specified trusts into LLPs, with automatic transfer of assets, liabilities, and ongoing proceedings upon registration.
- Clause 18: Expands the threshold limits for 'small companies' under Section 2(85) of the Companies Act to a paid-up capital not exceeding twenty crore rupees and turnover not exceeding two hundred crore rupees.
- Clause 24, 25, 47, 56, 75, 88 & 100: Replaces court-imposed fines and imprisonment with civil adjudicatory penalties for procedural defaults, including improper prospectus issuance, director duty defaults, and misrepresentation of 'Limited' or 'Private Limited'.
- Clause 32 & 34: Inserts Section 96(3) and Section 100(7) into the Companies Act, authorizing companies to conduct AGMs and EGMs virtually or through audio-visual hybrid means, while requiring a physical AGM at least once every three years.
- Clause 40 & 41: Establishes NFRA as a body corporate, creates the NFRA Fund, empowers it to issue directions and penalties to auditors, and provides regulations for registration intimation and return filing.
- Clause 43: Amends Section 135 to raise the CSR net profit eligibility threshold from five crore to ten crore rupees, extends the timeline to transfer unspent ongoing project funds to ninety days, and allows prescribed company classes to be exempted.
- Clause 51: Empowers the Central Government to verify, deactivate, or cancel Director Identification Numbers (DINs) under specified non-compliance or disqualification conditions.
- Clause 67 & 69: Directs merger and compromise applications under Sections 230–233 to the NCLT having jurisdiction over the transferee company, harmonizes shareholder approval thresholds to 75% of those present and voting, and clarifies that compromises under the Companies Act cannot override liquidation under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.
- Clause 70: Introduces Section 233A requiring companies holding their own shares (or shares held in trust) as a result of past compromises or arrangements to dispose of or cancel and extinguish them within three years.
- Clause 73: Designates the IBBI as the Valuation Authority to regulate valuers and registered valuers' organisations, recommend valuation standards, and handle misconduct penalties.
- Clause 103: Inserts Section 454B establishing a recovery mechanism for unpaid civil penalties (borrowing modes like attachment and arrest from the Income-tax Act, 1961), Section 454C providing a statutory settlement mechanism, and Section 454D requiring a mandatory 10% pre-deposit before appeals can be entertained.

## Fiscal or legal impact

The Financial Memorandum states that under proposed Section 132B, the Central Government may provide grants to the newly constituted National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA) Fund after due parliamentary appropriation; no other recurring or non-recurring expenditures are anticipated. Legally, the Bill transfers extensive procedural offences from criminal prosecution before magistrate courts to in-house civil penalty adjudication under appointed adjudicating officers and Regional Directors, with penalties and settlements credited to the Consolidated Fund of India. It also imposes a mandatory 10% pre-deposit requirement for filing appeals against orders of the NFRA, Valuation Authority, or adjudicating officers, introduces powers for recovery officers to attach property or bank accounts for unpaid penalties, and bars civil court jurisdiction over matters delegated to NFRA.
