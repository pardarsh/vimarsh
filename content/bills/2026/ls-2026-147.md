# THE BANKERS' BOOKS EVIDENCE BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Bankers’ Books Evidence Bill, 2026 updates the legal framework for admitting banking records as evidence in legal proceedings to reflect modern digital banking practices. It repeals the Bankers’ Books Evidence Act, 1891, laying down clear conditions and certification mechanisms for admitting both physical and electronic or digital records. It also provides protections for bank officers against routine compulsion to produce original records in proceedings where the bank is not a party.

## Summary

- Repeals the Bankers’ Books Evidence Act, 1891 and replaces it with updated rules aligned with contemporary electronic and cloud-based data storage.
- Expands the definition of 'bankers’ books' to include records kept in physical form or stored electronically, digitally, onsite, offsite, virtually, or in cloud locations, including backup and disaster recovery sites.
- Establishes certified copies of bankers' books as prima facie evidence of recorded entries, transactions, and accounts in all legal proceedings to the same extent as the original entries.
- Outlines specific conditions and mandatory certificate templates (First and Second Schedules) required to authenticate physical and digital bank records.
- Shields bank officers from being compelled to appear as witnesses or produce original bankers' books in proceedings where the bank is not a party, unless ordered by a court for 'special cause' recorded in writing.
- Empowers courts—or police officers not below the rank of Superintendent of Police in criminal investigations—to order the inspection or production of certified copies of bankers' books.
- Grants the Central Government the authority to extend the Act's provisions to other financial entities and to amend the certificate formats in the Schedules.

## Key provisions

- Clause 2(1)(b): Defines 'bankers' books' comprehensively to include physical ledgers and records stored in digital or electronic formats, offsite, virtually, or across cloud and disaster recovery sites.
- Clause 3 & Schedules: Mandates that copies of physical or electronic records be accompanied by specific certificates (First Schedule for physical, Second Schedule for digital) signed manually or electronically by an authorized bank officer.
- Clause 4: Enables the Central Government by notification to extend the application of the Act to the books of any entity or class of entities operating in the financial sector.
- Clause 5 & Clause 6: Confirms that certified copies are prima facie evidence of entries, and explicitly bars the denial of admissibility of banker's book records solely on the grounds that they are in electronic or digital form.
- Clause 7: Prescribes detailed technical and procedural conditions for the admissibility of electronic or digital records, including system integrity, regular feeding of data, authorized access, safeguards against cyber threats, and proper data transfer.
- Clause 8: Restricts courts from compelling bank officers to produce original books or appear as witnesses when the bank is not a party, except under 'special cause' (e.g., doubts about accuracy or disrupted record-keeping).
- Clause 9 & Clause 11: Authorizes courts to order inspection or production of certified copies upon application, and allows an officer not below the rank of Superintendent of Police to exercise equivalent powers during certain investigations.
- Clause 10: Allows courts to determine the costs of applications under the Act, including ordering banks to pay costs for improper delays or defaults, executable as civil money decrees.
- Clause 16: Formally repeals the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891 with standard savings for pending proceedings, previous operations, and accrued rights.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill modernizes evidentiary standards for bank records by recognizing cloud and electronic data, harmonizing terms with the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, and Information Technology Act, 2000, and repealing the Bankers’ Books Evidence Act, 1891. It streamlines how courts and law enforcement obtain bank evidence while protecting banking personnel from unnecessary court appearances. The text contains no Financial Memorandum and states no direct fiscal impact on the Consolidated Fund of India.
