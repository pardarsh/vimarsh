# THE JAN VISHWAS (AMENDMENT OF PROVISIONS) BILL, 2026

The Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Bill, 2026 amends 80 central enactments to decriminalise minor offences and rationalize penalties. It replaces imprisonment terms and criminal prosecutions with civil penalties, warnings, and administrative adjudication mechanisms. The bill also establishes periodic mandatory increases in monetary fines and penalties every three years.

## Summary

- Amends 80 individual central statutes across various sectors to substitute criminal imprisonment and court-imposed fines with administrative civil penalties.
- Establishes a statutory rule under Section 3 mandating an automatic 10 percent increase in minimum fines and penalties every three years from commencement, unless specific Acts prescribe alternative revision mechanisms.
- Sets up an administrative adjudication framework across numerous statutes, designating departmental adjudicating officers and departmental appellate authorities with defined timelines (typically requiring appeals within 30 days and disposal within 60 days).
- Introduces non-penal enforcement tools such as written warnings, advisories, and improvement notices for first-time or minor infractions under Acts such as the Legal Metrology Act, Apprentices Act, and New Delhi Municipal Council Act.
- Overhauls the municipal property tax assessment and penalty framework under the New Delhi Municipal Council Act, 1994, instituting a base unit area value system and establishing a Municipal Valuation Committee and a Hardship and Anomaly Committee.
- Updates references to older criminal procedural codes and penal statutes across amended enactments to align with the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 and the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023.
- Expands provisions for compounding of specified offences across maritime, ports, electricity, and shipping laws.
- Includes standard savings provisions and grants the Central Government power to remove difficulties within two years of commencement through orders laid before Parliament.

## Key provisions

- Section 3: Mandates an automatic 10% increase of the minimum prescribed fines and penalties across covered enactments after every three years.
- Schedule Item 1 (Court-fees Act, 1870): Divides rule violations into those with fraudulent intent (up to 6 months imprisonment or fine up to Rs 10,000) and those without fraudulent intent (civil penalty up to Rs 10,000 via administrative adjudication).
- Schedule Item 8 (Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940): Decriminalises certain offences regarding cosmetics and disclosure of information by replacing imprisonment with financial penalties, and creates an administrative adjudicating mechanism.
- Schedule Item 26 & 53 (Delhi Municipal Corporation Act, 1957 & New Delhi Municipal Council Act, 1994): Replaces criminal prosecution for various civic and building contraventions with structured civil penalty schedules; restructures NDMC property tax on a unit area valuation system.
- Schedule Item 29 (Apprentices Act, 1961): Replaces penalties for first and second contraventions by employers with mandatory advisories and warnings, applying monetary penalties only for subsequent non-compliance.
- Schedule Item 51 (Motor Vehicles Act, 1988): Eliminates imprisonment for various non-safety violations, introduces formal warnings for noise/regulation breaches, and extends driving licence validity buffers and compensation application periods.
- Schedule Item 52 (Railways Act, 1989): Adjusts punishments for ticketless travel, nuisance, and trespassing, establishing fixed penalties and directing matters to court primarily upon refusal to pay.
- Schedule Item 66 (Legal Metrology Act, 2009): Introduces 'improvement notices' for initial offences under numerous sections instead of immediate fines or prosecutions, and expands compounding mechanisms.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill shifts hundreds of infractions from criminal courts to administrative adjudicating officers, decriminalising conduct and reducing judicial case loads while standardising appeal channels and referencing current penal codes (Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita and Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita). Unpaid penalties are routinely made recoverable as arrears of land revenue. Fiscally, the bill raises various monetary penalties, mandates an automatic 10 percent escalation every three years, and redirects certain penalties (such as under the Cattle-trespass Act and animal disease laws) directly to statutory funds like the Animal Welfare Board of India. No separate Financial Memorandum detailing administrative costs was included in the bill text.
