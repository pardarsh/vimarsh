# THE TRIBUNALS REFORMS BILL, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026 establishes a National Tribunals Commission to streamline and oversee the appointment, service conditions, performance, and conduct of members and chairpersons across key tribunals. It sets uniform five-year tenures, caps retirement ages, and introduces Search-cum-Selection Committees for tribunal appointments. The bill also repeals the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021 and amends multiple sector-specific enactments to bring their tribunal appointments under the new framework.

## Summary

- Establishes the National Tribunals Commission headquartered in New Delhi, composed of a Chairperson (former Supreme Court Judge or High Court Chief Justice), two Judicial Members, and two Technical Members (Clause 3).
- Empowers the Commission to oversee tribunal selections, review performance, submit annual reports, conduct inquiries into complaints against tribunal members, and maintain the National Tribunals Data Grid (Clause 4).
- Requires Search-cum-Selection Committees to recommend candidate panels and waiting lists for tribunal appointments, which the Central Government must process within three months (Clauses 13 and 14).
- Standardizes terms of office for tribunal heads and members to five years, with an upper age limit of 70 years for Chairpersons and 67 years for Members, alongside eligibility for reappointment (Clause 17).
- Outlines specific grounds and inquiry mechanisms for the removal of Commission and Tribunal members (Clauses 6 and 16).
- Repeals the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021 while preserving past valid actions, pending selections, and protecting existing appointees' terms from prejudicial variations (Clause 24).
- Consequentially amends 14 major statutes—including the Customs Act, SEBI Act, Electricity Act, and Companies Act—to align their tribunal appointments with this Act (Clause 18, Second Schedule).

## Key provisions

- Clause 3: Establishes the National Tribunals Commission consisting of a Chairperson and four Members (two Judicial, two Technical).
- Clause 4: Tasks the Commission with managing tribunal selections, reviewing performance, investigating complaints, and running the National Tribunals Data Grid.
- Clause 13: Mandates the composition of Search-cum-Selection Committees for appointing Chairpersons and Members of covered tribunals.
- Clause 14(8): Imposes a binding timeline directing the Central Government to make appointments within three months of receiving recommendations.
- Clause 16: Sets standard criteria for removing tribunal members (insolvency, conviction, incapacity, bias, misbehavior, incompetence) through an inquiry by the Commission.
- Clause 17: Fixes tribunal Chairperson tenures at 5 years or up to 70 years of age, and Member tenures at 5 years or up to 67 years of age.
- Clause 24: Repeals the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021 while inserting transition and savings clauses for ongoing processes and sitting members.
- First & Second Schedules: Enumerate 16 covered tribunals and directly amend their parent statutes to subject qualifications, appointments, and service conditions to this Act.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the Act establishes overriding authority over qualifications, tenures, and service terms across 16 major tribunals specified in the First Schedule, amending relevant provisions in acts like the Customs Act, Companies Act, and SEBI Act, while repealing the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021. Fiscally, the Central Government will fund the Commission through parliamentary grants to cover administrative operations, salaries, and allowances of the Commission and its Secretariat (Clause 10), with accounts subject to audit by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (Clause 11).
