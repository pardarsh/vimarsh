# The Central Armed Police Forces (General Administration) Bill, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Central Armed Police Forces (General Administration) Bill, 2026 establishes an overarching legal framework to regulate the recruitment and service conditions of Group A General Duty Officers and other officers across Central Armed Police Forces. It mandates specific deputation quotas for Indian Police Service officers in senior leadership positions, including fifty percent at the Inspector General level and all posts at the Special DG and DG levels. The bill gives these rules overriding effect over any contrary laws, judgments, or prior orders while preserving existing financial benefits.

## Summary

- Serves as an umbrella statute governing the recruitment, promotion, deputation, and service conditions of officers in five key Central Armed Police Forces: CRPF, BSF, CISF, ITBP, and SSB (Clause 1 and First Schedule).
- Empowers the Central Government to formulate rules governing methods of recruitment and service conditions, overriding any existing laws, court judgments, decrees, or official orders (Clause 3(1)).
- Establishes statutory reservation quotas for Indian Police Service (IPS) officers on deputation in senior CAPF leadership ranks (Clause 3(1)).
- Provides that the rules will prevail in case of any inconsistency with prior or subsequent rules and orders (Clause 3(3)).
- Authorizes the Central Government to amend the First and Second Schedules via official notification to include other armed forces or rules (Clause 4).
- Protects past financial benefits granted to Group A General Duty Officers until fresh orders are issued (Clause 5).
- Gives the provisions of the Act overriding effect over inconsistent laws or instruments (Clause 7) and provides a three-year window to remove implementation difficulties (Clause 8).

## Key provisions

- Clause 3(1): Authorizes the Central Government to notify rules for recruitment, promotion, and service conditions notwithstanding court judgments, decrees, or orders.
- Clause 3(1) Provisos: Mandates that 50% of Inspector General posts and at least 67% of Additional Director General posts be filled by deputation from the Indian Police Service, while all posts of Special Director General and Director General must be filled exclusively by deputation.
- Clause 3(3) and Clause 7: Establishes a non-obstante and supremacy clause giving the Act and its rules precedence over any inconsistent laws, rules, or orders.
- Clause 4: Empowers the Central Government to amend the First Schedule (covered armed forces) and the Second Schedule (applicable cadre rules) by notification subject to parliamentary tabling.
- Clause 5: Saves existing financial benefits awarded to Group A General Duty Officers under prior orders until superseded by new orders.
- Clause 8: Grants the Central Government power to issue orders to remove implementation difficulties within three years of commencement.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill creates an overriding statutory standard for CAPF officer recruitment and postings that supersedes contrary judicial decrees, court judgments, and previous administrative orders. It formally reserves the topmost leadership roles (DG and Special DG) exclusively for deputation, and sets minimum deputation quotas of 50% and 67% for IG and ADG ranks, respectively. Fiscally, the bill specifies no new appropriations and contains no Financial Memorandum in the text; Clause 5 ensures that existing financial benefits already granted to Group A General Duty Officers remain protected until new orders are issued.
