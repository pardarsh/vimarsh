# The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (Amendment) Bill, 2026

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (Amendment) Bill, 2026 updates the criteria and digital registration framework for MSMEs. It mandates the settlement of MSME invoices by Central Public Sector Enterprises through the Trade Receivables Discounting System (TReDS) and introduces strict timelines for dispute mediation and arbitration. Additionally, it strengthens the enforcement of arbitral awards and establishes an administrative penalty mechanism for violations.

## Summary

- Revises classification criteria for MSMEs under Section 7 based on both investment in plant, machinery, or equipment and turnover, explicitly excluding costs for pollution control, R&D, and safety devices from investment calculations.
- Provides for the notification of a national digital platform (and optional state platforms) for free and voluntary filing of enterprise registration memorandums (Section 8).
- Mandates Central Public Sector Enterprises (and allows governments to mandate other entities) to route and settle MSME procurement invoices through an RBI-authorised TReDS platform (Section 15A) and report compliance (Section 22A).
- Imposes strict timelines for dispute resolution: 90 days from first appearance for mediation, 30 days post-mediation to initiate reference, and 90 days from the completion of pleadings to deliver an arbitral award (Section 18).
- Authorises online mechanisms for conducting mediation and arbitration, and confirms jurisdiction lies where the supplier is registered against buyers anywhere in India (Section 18).
- Makes mediated settlement agreements and arbitral awards enforceable as arrears of land revenue and explicitly recognises them as valid and enforceable debts under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (Section 18A).
- Requires buyers challenging awards to deposit 75% of the amount, with a mandatory interim payout of at least 50% to the supplier if the court application is pending for more than six months (Section 19).
- Restructures Facilitation Councils to include 3 to 5 members (chaired by an officer not below Joint Director, with MSME association representatives and at least one legal expert) and empowers states to set up additional councils (Sections 20, 21).
- Revises penal provisions by replacing direct criminal prosecution with a tiered warning and penalty structure adjudicated by the Development Commissioner, with an appeal to the Central Ministry Secretary (Sections 27, 27A).

## Key provisions

- Clause 4 (Section 7): Classifies MSMEs based on twin criteria of investment and turnover, excluding expenses on pollution control, R&D, and industrial safety devices from investment computations.
- Clause 5 (Section 8): Mandates a national digital platform for free, voluntary MSME registration and permits state governments to establish state-level platforms.
- Clause 7 (Section 15A): Compulsorily routes settlement of invoices for CPSEs procuring from MSMEs through RBI-authorised TReDS platforms, with enabling powers for other entities.
- Clause 8 (Section 18): Imposes a 90-day cap for mediation, a 90-day deadline from close of pleadings for arbitral awards, supplier-location jurisdiction across India, and enables online dispute resolution.
- Clause 9 (Section 18A): Provides for recovery of mediated settlements and arbitral awards as arrears of land revenue, and categorises them as debts under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.
- Clause 10 (Section 19): Mandates a 75% pre-deposit by non-suppliers to challenge awards, requires filing in the supplier's court jurisdiction, and directs payment of at least 50% of the award to suppliers if the setting-aside challenge exceeds six months.
- Clause 11 & 12 (Sections 20 and 21): Directs states to establish adequate Facilitation Councils comprising 3 to 5 members, including a Joint Director-level Chairperson, industry representatives, and a legal expert.
- Clause 13 (Section 22A): Imposes mandatory disclosure and reporting on invoices routed through TReDS platforms.
- Clause 14 (Sections 27 and 27A): Establishes a graded penalty structure (warnings for first offenses, escalating fines thereafter, with a 10% increase every three years) adjudicated by the Development Commissioner, with a 30-day appeal window to the Ministry Secretary.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the bill shortens arbitration and mediation timelines, formalises supplier-based territorial jurisdiction, streamlines recovery by treating awards as arrears of land revenue and debts under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, and decodes non-compliance into an administrative adjudication system under the Development Commissioner. Fiscally, the text does not quantify direct central budget expenditure; it requires State Governments to establish adequate Facilitation Councils and provide infrastructure, systems, and manpower, and empowers governments to recover unpaid penalties and awards as arrears of land revenue.
