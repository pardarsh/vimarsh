# THE DELIMITATION BILL, 2026.

> This analysis was generated automatically by a language model from the official bill text. It may contain errors. Read the official text before relying on it.

The Delimitation Bill, 2026 provides for the establishment of a Delimitation Commission to readjust Lok Sabha and Legislative Assembly constituencies based on the latest published census figures. It establishes the Commission's composition, powers, and procedures, notably mandating one-third reservation of seats for women to be allocated by rotation. It also repeals the Delimitation Act, 2002.

## Summary

- Empowers the Central Government to establish a Delimitation Commission chaired by a retired or serving Supreme Court Judge, with the Chief Election Commissioner (or an Election Commissioner) and respective State Election Commissioners as ex officio members.
- Assigns up to ten associate members per State (five Lok Sabha MPs and five MLAs nominated by their respective Speakers) without voting rights or decision-signing authority.
- Mandates the readjustment of seat allocations and division of territorial constituencies for the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies using the latest published census data.
- Implements statutory reservation of approximately one-third of total seats for women—including Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe women—allotted by rotation among constituencies.
- Requires Legislative Assembly seats in any State to be an integral multiple of that State's Lok Sabha seats, with Assembly constituencies falling wholly inside one Parliamentary constituency.
- Invests the Commission with the powers of a civil court for summoning witnesses, requiring documents, and requisitioning public records.
- Outlines public consultation processes, requiring draft proposals to be published for objections and public hearings before final orders are issued.
- Grants final delimitation orders the force of law upon publication in the Gazette of India, barring them from being questioned in any court.
- Repeals the Delimitation Act, 2002, and specifies that current representation remains intact until the dissolution of existing Houses.

## Key provisions

- Clause 3: Authorises the constitution of a Delimitation Commission comprising a Supreme Court Judge (serving or retired) as Chairperson, the CEC or a nominated EC, and the relevant State Election Commissioner; directs the EC to act as the Commission for Pakistan-occupied Jammu and Kashmir constituencies when vacated.
- Clause 5: Provides for ten associate members per State (five MPs and five MLAs) who cannot vote or sign decisions, and empowers the Commission to seek technical assistance from census, survey, and GIS experts.
- Clause 7: Grants the Commission civil court powers under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, with majority decisions prevailing.
- Clause 8: Mandates the Commission to fix seat allocations, SC/ST reservations, and a one-third reservation for women (rotated among constituencies, including rotation within SC/ST seats).
- Clause 9: Details delimitation criteria, including geographically compact areas, adherence to administrative boundaries, single-member constituencies, publication of draft orders, and mandatory public sittings.
- Clause 10: Bestows final orders with the force of law upon Gazette publication, insulates them from judicial review, and applies them to all elections post-dissolution of current Houses.
- Clause 11: Authorises the Election Commission to update clerical errors and local administrative boundary name changes without altering constituency limits.
- Clause 13: Repeals the Delimitation Act, 2002.

## Fiscal or legal impact

Legally, the Bill repeals the Delimitation Act, 2002, gives its own provisions overriding effect over conflicting laws, and protects final delimitation orders published in the Gazette of India from being questioned in any court. According to the Financial Memorandum, the costs of establishing and running the Commission—including salaries, allowances, and delimitation expenses—will be funded from the Consolidated Fund of India and borne by the Election Commission of India; however, a precise estimate of total expenditure cannot be determined at this stage.
